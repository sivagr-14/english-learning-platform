## pilot01-0001 · rationale
/ˌræʃəˈnæl/ · noun
**Meaning:** the reasons that explain a decision or plan
**Tamil:** முடிவிற்கான காரண விளக்கம்
**Quick memory cue:** The WHY behind a decision.

### Overview

**Meaning usage profile**
**Meaning type:** Abstract or practical concept: the reasons that explain a decision or plan

**Connotation:** Neutral in itself: the situation determines whether the result is welcome.

**Tone:** Measured and explanatory; suitable for discussing plans or judgments.

**Register:** Neutral to professional; also usable in ordinary planning and discussion.


### Meaning in Context

**Source sentence:** What is the rationale for postponing the launch?

**Contextual meaning:** the reasons that explain a decision or plan

**Simple explanation:** The WHY behind a decision. Here, the reasons that explain a decision or plan.


### Usage Guide

**When to use**
- Use “rationale” for this meaning: the reasons that explain a decision or plan. For a concrete situation: What is the rationale for postponing the launch?
**When not to use**
- A rationale explains why a choice was made; a goal says what it should achieve.

### Patterns & Collocations

**Main pattern:** rationale: the rationale for + noun/-ing

**Common collocations**
- a clear rationale
- explain the rationale

### Natural Examples

**Examples**
**Workplace:** What is the rationale for postponing the launch?

**Another context:** The doctor explained the rationale for changing my appointment schedule.

**Mini conversation:** A: What is the rationale for postponing the launch?
B: A later launch gives us time to check the payment process.


### Mistakes & Differences

**Common mistake:** What is the rationale of postpone it?

**Correction:** What is the rationale for postponing it?

**Important difference:** A rationale explains why a choice was made; a goal says what it should achieve.


### Memory & Practice

**Memory trigger:** The WHY behind a decision.

**Memory sentence:** The doctor explained the rationale for changing my appointment schedule.

**Recall question:** Without looking at the heading, recall the expression meaning “the reasons that explain a decision or plan”. Then explain it in your own words.

**Recognition task:** Compare these two statements and choose the accurate form or interpretation. A: “What is the rationale for postponing it?” B: “What is the rationale of postpone it?” Explain the difference before checking Mistakes & Differences.

**Production task:** Use “rationale” in your own response: Explain why a team might trial a change before introducing it everywhere.


### Advanced Nuance

- rationale: A rationale can be coherent without being persuasive; ask whether its assumptions are supported.

### Mistakes & Differences

**Common mistake:** The lost contract is just a bump in the road, even if it closes the business.

**Correction:** Use bump in the road for a recoverable setback, not to dismiss a potentially decisive loss.

**Important difference:** A bump in the road suggests a temporary setback; a dead end suggests that no further progress is possible along that route.
