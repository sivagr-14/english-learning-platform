## pilot01-0101 · setback
/ˈsetbæk/ · noun
**Meaning:** a problem that delays or reverses progress
**Tamil:** முன்னேற்றத்தை தாமதப்படுத்தும் பின்னடைவு
**Quick memory cue:** One step backwards on a longer route.

### Overview

**Meaning usage profile**
**Meaning type:** Abstract or practical concept: a problem that delays or reverses progress

**Connotation:** Neutral in itself: the situation determines whether the result is welcome.

**Tone:** Measured and explanatory; suitable for discussing plans or judgments.

**Register:** Neutral to professional; also usable in ordinary planning and discussion.

### Meaning in Context

**Source sentence:** Losing the venue was a setback, but we found another hall.

**Contextual meaning:** a problem that delays or reverses progress

**Simple explanation:** One step backwards on a longer route. Here, a problem that delays or reverses progress.

### Usage Guide

**When to use**
- Use “setback” for this meaning: a problem that delays or reverses progress. For a concrete situation: Losing the venue was a setback, but we found another hall.
**When not to use**
- A setback reverses or delays progress; a blocker prevents progress altogether until it is removed.

### Patterns & Collocations

**Main pattern:** setback: a setback for/to + noun; suffer a setback

**Common collocations**
- a temporary setback
- a major setback

### Natural Examples

**Examples**
**workplace:** Losing the venue was a setback, but we found another hall.

**another context:** Failing my first driving test was a setback, but it showed me what to practise.

**Mini conversation:** A: Losing the venue was a setback, but we found another hall.
B: It delays us, but it does not make the goal impossible.

### Mistakes & Differences

**Common mistake:** We had a setback on our progress.

**Correction:** We suffered a setback in our progress.

**Important difference:** A setback reverses or delays progress; a blocker prevents progress altogether until it is removed.

### Memory & Practice

**Memory trigger:** One step backwards on a longer route.

**Memory sentence:** Failing my first driving test was a setback, but it showed me what to practise.

**Recall question:** Without looking at the heading, recall the expression meaning “a problem that delays or reverses progress”. Then explain it in your own words.

**Recognition task:** Compare these two statements and choose the accurate form or interpretation. A: “We suffered a setback in our progress.” B: “We had a setback on our progress.” Explain the difference before checking Mistakes & Differences.

**Production task:** Use “setback” in your own response: Describe a recent problem that slowed a plan and the next recovery step.

### Advanced Nuance

- setback: A setback can be serious or temporary, so describe its actual effect instead of assuming the project has failed.
